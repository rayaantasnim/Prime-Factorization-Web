01

Strategy 01: Parity & Modulo 5 Isolation

Immediate extraction of powers of 2 and 5 by terminal digit inspection

Case Study 1.1

Even Composite Stripping

360 → 2³ × 45

When the last digit is {0, 2, 4, 6, 8}, divide continuously by 2. 360 → 180 → 90 → 45. Stripping 2³ drops the composite from 360 to an odd 45 in three quick mental operations.

Case Study 1.2

Terminal 5 Factor Extraction

875 → 5³ × 7

Any integer ending in 5 contains 5 as a divisor. 875 ÷ 5 = 175; 175 ÷ 5 = 35; 35 ÷ 5 = 7. Three consecutive divisions leave the single prime 7.

Case Study 1.3

Decadic Zero Pairing (10 = 2 × 5)

4,200 → 2² × 5² × 42

Every trailing zero represents a factor of 10 = 2 × 5. Two trailing zeros in 4,200 immediately provide 2² × 5², leaving 42 = 2 × 3 × 7.

Case Study 1.4

Odd Non-5 Quotient Isolation

1,020 → 2² × 5 × 51

Dividing 1,020 by 20 yields 51. Since 51 ends in 1, it has zero factors of 2 and 5. This allows immediate transition to Modulo 3 and digit sum tests.

02

Strategy 02: Sum of Digits & Modulo 9

Digital root heuristics for rapid identification of 3 and 3² = 9 divisors

Case Study 2.1

Modulo 9 Digital Root Test

1,458 → 1+4+5+8 = 18

18 is divisible by 9, proving 1,458 contains 9. 1,458 ÷ 9 = 162. Digital sum of 162 is 1+6+2 = 9, so 162 ÷ 9 = 18. Thus 1,458 = 2 × 3⁶.

Case Study 2.2

Modulo 3 Single Extraction

438 → 4+3+8 = 15

15 mod 9 = 6 ≠ 0, but 15 is divisible by 3. This guarantees exactly one initial factor of 3. 438 ÷ 3 = 146. Parity reveals 146 ÷ 2 = 73 (prime).

Case Study 2.3

Casting Out Nines Shortcut

9,522 → (9) + 5 + 2 + 2 = 9

Cast out digits summing to 9 to accelerate mental checks: ignore 9, then 5+2+2 = 9. 9,522 is divisible by 9 → 9,522 ÷ 9 = 1,058 = 2 × 23².

Case Study 2.4

Fast Elimination of Non-Multiples

1,217 → 1+2+1+7 = 11

11 mod 3 = 2. You instantly eliminate 3 and 9 from all factorization trees without ever attempting trial division. Move directly to primes ≥ 7.

03

Strategy 03: Difference of Squares (a² - b²)

Fermat factorization of odd composites via algebraic difference of squares

Case Study 3.1

Nearby Square Decomposition

899 = 30² - 1²

899 is one less than 900 = 30². By the identity a² - b² = (a-b)(a+b), 899 = (30 - 1)(30 + 1) = 29 × 31. Solved in 2 seconds without trial division.

Case Study 3.2

Two-Step Square Proximity

2,496 = 50² - 2²

2,496 is four less than 2,500 = 50². Using a² - b², 2,496 = (50 - 2)(50 + 2) = 48 × 52. Further factor 48 = 2⁴ × 3 and 52 = 2² × 13 → 2⁶ × 3 × 13.

Case Study 3.3

Twin Prime Factorization Clue

3,599 = 60² - 1²

3,599 is one less than 3,600 = 60². Therefore 3,599 = (60 - 1)(60 + 1) = 59 × 61. Both 59 and 61 are prime numbers, completing the decomposition.

Case Study 3.4

Fermat Iterative Testing

1,517 + 6² = 39²

Ceiling root of 1,517 is 39 (39² = 1,521). 1,521 - 1,517 = 4 = 2². Thus 1,517 = 39² - 2² = (39 - 2)(39 + 2) = 37 × 41. Solved on the first Fermat step.
